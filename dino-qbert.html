How it works

DinoQbert recreates the 1982 Gottlieb arcade original: hop-based movement, one jump per keypress with a 150ms arc animation, and the classic @!#?@! swear bubble on death. Coily spawns as an egg, bounces randomly down the pyramid, then hatches and chases the player. Dark balls bounce down and kill on contact; light balls are safe to catch for 100 pts. Slick (round 4+) hops down uncoloring cubes. Two transport discs at variable positions let Q*bert escape and lure Coily to his death.

Tap Viz to reveal the AI’s plan: a fading line traces the MCTS best path, with dots shrinking to show decreasing confidence at deeper steps. Faint dots mark remaining uncolored cubes. The AI uses Monte Carlo Tree Search (200 iterations per move, depth 8): it clones the full game state—player, cubes, all enemies—then runs hundreds of random rollouts. Coily moves deterministically (greedy chase) while all other enemies (cacti, evil dino) move randomly in each simulation, so MCTS naturally handles the stochastic environment. Rollouts are biased toward uncolored cubes. A hard-coded disc escape fires when Coily is within 2 hops of a catch position.
