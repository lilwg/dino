How it works

DinoQbert recreates the 1982 Gottlieb arcade original: hop-based movement, one jump per keypress with arcade-matched hop timing (~0.67s per hop), and the classic @!#?@! swear bubble on death. Coily spawns as an egg, bounces randomly down the pyramid, then hatches and chases the player. Dark balls bounce down and kill on contact; light balls are safe to catch for 100 pts. Slick (round 4+) hops down uncoloring cubes. Ugg and Wrongway (round 3+) crawl upward from the bottom corners. Two transport discs at variable positions let Q*bert escape and lure Coily to his death.

Tap Viz to see the AI’s decision-making: colored dots show scored directions (green=best, red=worst), a dashed arrow points to the chosen move, and faint dots mark uncolored cubes. The AI has two modes. Mode 1 (no Coily): Dijkstra tour planning picks the nearest unfinished cube while avoiding a danger set built from enemy positions and predicted paths. Mode 2 (Coily active): Monte Carlo search runs 24 forward simulations per direction, 6 hops deep, using a greedy rollout policy that exploits Coily’s deterministic chase. It picks the move with the highest survival rate, breaking ties by tour cost (MST heuristic). Coily always chases the player’s source tile during a jump, so jumping toward a standing Coily is safe—they swap tiles at apex. The AI also evaluates disc lures to kill Coily when he’ll chase off the edge.
