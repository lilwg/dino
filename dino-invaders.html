How it works

The AI uses Monte Carlo forward simulation. Each frame it snapshots the game state and, for each of six actions (left, right, fire, fire-left, fire-right, stay), runs five random rollouts 45 frames into the future — this time with enemy fire enabled, so the AI must account for incoming bullets it hasn’t seen yet. Each rollout is scored purely on outcomes: points gained, deaths, and closest bullet distance. The scores are averaged across rollouts and the best action is chosen. No hand-tuned positional rules — the simulation itself naturally penalises bad positions because they lead to getting hit. Stay wins ties, keeping the player conservative when nothing is urgent.

Tap Viz to watch the AI think. Coloured trails show predicted player positions — blue for left, orange for right, grey for staying still. A dashed line traces the player bullet’s path for the chosen action. Pill badges above the trails show each family’s outcome (hit, score, or safe), and a badge below the player names the chosen action.
